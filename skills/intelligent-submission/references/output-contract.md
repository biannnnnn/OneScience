# Submission strategy report

Write for decision-making, not as a generic literature review. Keep sourced facts and model judgments visibly distinct.

## Recommended structure

1. **Recommendation summary**: ordered submission strategy and the main trade-off behind each position.
2. **Manuscript profile**: contribution, methods, evidence, key topics, and important limitations inferred from the provided text.
3. **Candidate comparison**: a compact table with journal, fit rationale, verified constraints, reference count, manuscript score, recent median or range, delta, benchmark band, and confidence.
4. **Journal details**: for each candidate, cite scope/policy evidence and list the recent comparable papers with persistent links. Explain why they are comparable.
5. **Revision priorities**: changes most likely to improve evidence, positioning, clarity, or compliance before submission. Tie each suggestion to an observed gap.
6. **Uncertainty and method note**: disclose missing abstracts or policies, sparse baselines, retrieval limitations, and the use of experimental LLM scoring.

## Interpretation rules

- Order journals by the combined submission strategy: fit, user constraints, within-journal benchmark, and uncertainty. Never order journals by raw manuscript scores from separate batches.
- Describe the benchmark as `above`, `near`, or `below the retrieved recent-paper baseline`; do not say `will be accepted`, `acceptance chance`, or equivalent.
- Use challenge/balanced/broad-reach as portfolio labels, not probability bands.
- Include the observation date for volatile facts such as fees, metrics, and policies.
- Cite every current journal fact and every retrieved paper. A model-generated score or inference should be labeled as such and does not need a fabricated citation.
- If evidence is insufficient, recommend what to verify next rather than forcing a ranked conclusion.

End with this meaning in the user's language:

> The comparison is generated from public metadata, retrieved abstracts, and experimental LLM judgment. It is not peer review or a calibrated acceptance probability; journal policies and charges must be confirmed before submission.
